Privacy Policy

Effective 23 October 2026 App Store: Thomas Baxter Google Play: Flair Technologies Ltd Applies to Taken for iPhone and Android

The short versionTaken collects no data, on iPhone or Android. Everything you log stays on your phone, and a voice note is turned into to-dos on the phone itself. We never see it, store it, sell it or share it, because it never reaches us.

01Who we are

Taken is a pill and supplement log for iPhone and Android. On Apple's App Store it is published by Thomas Baxter. On Google Play it is published by Flair Technologies Ltd, a UK company. In this policy, we and us mean the publisher of the copy of Taken you use. This policy covers the Taken app on iPhone and Android, its widgets, its Control Center controls, its Siri and Shortcuts actions, its Android Quick Settings tile, and this website.

Sections 03 to 11 describe Taken for iPhone. Taken for Android keeps the same things on your phone and works the same way; section 12 says where it differs. For any question about this policy, email tjpb2@cantab.ac.uk.

02Data we collect

None. Taken's own code does not collect, transmit, sell or share any personal data, health data, usage data or device identifiers, on iPhone or Android. We do not operate any server that receives information from the app. On Android, Google's Play Billing Library, which Taken includes to sell Taken Pro, reports to Google how its own calls went (section 12). When you buy Taken Pro on Google Play, Google, not Taken, gives us a sales record of that order; section 12 says what it holds.

03What the app stores on your iPhone

To do its job, Taken keeps the following on your device:

  • the items in your stack: names, short code tags, schedules and order;
  • the date and time of each dose you log;
  • your settings: reminder on or off and its time, the trial reminder on or off, your wake-up time, haptics, the look you chose, and whether you answered Today's one-time offers (and the day you closed its line about yesterday, if you did);
  • your profile, if you set one up: the name you type and the photo you choose, kept as a small square copy;
  • your to-dos: the words of each one, when it is due if you said, and when you finished it (never the audio of a voice note), and the date of your first voice note;
  • your weigh-ins, if you use the optional Weight tab: the weight and the date and time of each one, and, if you set one, your weight goal with the height you entered for it; and the tab's settings: whether it is on, kilograms or pounds, the chart range you picked, whether you answered Today's offer to add it, and the date of your first weigh-in;
  • a small Taken Pro status file: whether Pro is active as Apple's App Store last reported it (the plan, the plan it renews to, and when it renews or ends), the plan prices the App Store last gave, when you first logged a dose, whether the Taken Pro offer and the notification question after a trial starts have been shown, and when the reminder before a trial ends was set to arrive.

This information is stored only on your iPhone, in the app's private storage and in an App Group container that Taken shares with its own widgets and Siri actions so they can show your progress and log doses. It is never sent to us or to anyone else.

A profile photo is one you pick in Apple's photo picker, which hands Taken that one photo. Taken never asks for access to your photo library.

04Voice to-dos

When you tap the mic in the To-do tab, Taken uses the microphone and Apple's on-device speech recognition to turn what you say into text, then sorts that text into to-dos with Apple's on-device model (Apple Intelligence, on iPhones that have it). Speech and task sorting happen on your iPhone. To hear names right, Taken gives Apple's on-device speech recognition up to 100 names, from your stack and its list of common supplements, as spelling hints. They are never stored or sent. The audio is never stored and never sent anywhere, and Taken never sends the words. A shortcut you build can (section 08). The words are kept only as your to-dos, on your iPhone, as listed above: while a note is being sorted it is saved as one to-do, so nothing you said is lost if Taken is closed, and then replaced by the to-dos it was sorted into.

Taken asks for microphone and speech recognition permission the first time you tap the mic, and listens only while the mic is on. You can turn either permission off at any time in iOS Settings, and type your to-dos instead. iOS may download Apple's speech model for your language once; that download comes from Apple and carries nothing you said.

05What we do not do

  • No accounts and no sign-in.
  • No analytics, no crash reporting services and no usage tracking.
  • No advertising and no advertising identifiers.
  • No third-party SDKs or libraries of any kind.
  • No network requests of our own. Taken works offline; to buy or check Taken Pro, iOS contacts Apple's App Store.
  • No tracking across other companies' apps or websites.

06Purchases

Taken Pro is bought through Apple's App Store with your Apple Account. Apple processes the payment under Apple's own privacy policy; we never see your payment details or your Apple Account.

To unlock Pro, Taken asks Apple's StoreKit on your iPhone whether there is an active Taken Pro purchase on your Apple Account, and which version of Taken you first downloaded (everyone whose first download was Taken 1.0 keeps Pro for life). iOS gets that answer from the App Store. Taken keeps only the result on your iPhone, in the status file listed above, so its widgets, Control Center control and Siri actions work without asking again. Nothing about your purchases reaches us, and they are never linked to your log.

07Notifications

If you turn on the nudge, Taken schedules local notifications on your iPhone. They are created and delivered by iOS on your device. No push service and no server is involved. Once notifications are allowed, a to-do with a time also gets a local notification at that time, and during a Taken Pro trial one reminder arrives at least 3 days before it ends (while notifications are off, Today shows it instead). Taken asks for notification permission only when you turn the nudge on, in your profile or from the one-time offer on Today, or once right after a trial starts. You can turn the nudge and the trial reminder off at any time in your profile, or every notification from Taken in iOS Settings.

08Siri, Shortcuts and widgets

Widgets, the Control Center control and Siri or Shortcuts actions read and update the same on-device log. When you speak to Siri, Apple processes your request under Apple's own privacy policy. Taken only receives the action you asked for, such as logging your stack, and answers from the log stored on your iPhone. New voice to-do, from Siri, Shortcuts, the Action button, Control Center, the Lock Screen or the app icon, only opens Taken; listening starts on screen, in the app.

Did I take it? and Get to-dos hand their answer to the shortcut that runs them. If your shortcut passes it to Use Model (On-Device, Private Cloud Compute or ChatGPT as an extension) or to another app, that service's terms apply; Taken itself sends nothing. On a locked iPhone, Did I take it? and Get to-dos ask for Face ID or your passcode before Siri answers. Take all from a locked iPhone replies with the names it logged.

09CSV export

Taken creates a CSV file of your log only when you choose Export CSV, and a CSV file of your weigh-ins only when you choose Export weight CSV. You decide where it goes using the iOS share sheet. Once you share the file, the app or person you share it with handles it under their own terms.

Import weight CSV reads only the file you pick in Files and adds the weigh-ins it finds to your log on your iPhone. Taken keeps the weigh-ins, not the file.

10Backups

If you use iCloud Backup or back up your iPhone to a computer, iOS may include Taken's data in that backup, as it does for most apps. Backups are handled by Apple and by you. We have no access to them.

11Deleting your data

You are always in control. In Taken, open your profile (the avatar at the top right) and choose Erase all data to delete your stack, your log, your to-dos, your weigh-ins, your weight goal and the height entered for it, your profile and your settings; the look you chose, whether you already answered Today's one-time offers (the nudge offer, the widget invite and the Weight tab offer), the Taken Pro status file, the date of your first voice note, and the Weight tab's on or off setting, its unit and the date of your first weigh-in, described above, stay. Deleting the app from your iPhone deletes everything it stored on the device, including the shared container used by its widgets. A Taken Pro purchase belongs to your Apple Account, and Restore brings it back.

12Taken for Android

Taken for Android (Android 9 and later, from Google Play) keeps the same things as Taken for iPhone (section 03) in the app's private storage on your phone, which other apps cannot read. Its home-screen widgets and its Quick Settings tile run inside the app and read the same files. None of it is sent to us or to anyone else. These are the differences.

Google Play Billing. Taken Pro is bought through Google Play with your Google account. Google processes the payment under the Google Privacy Policy; we never see your payment details or your Google account. Each time Taken opens or you come back to it, it asks Google Play on your phone which Taken Pro purchases your Google account holds. It keeps the answer on your phone, in the status file described in section 03: whether Pro is active, the plan, whether it renews, and when Google Play last answered. The file also keeps the plan prices Google Play last showed, and the bookkeeping listed in section 03, such as when you first logged a dose. If a widget, the tile or the nudge could not log a dose without Pro, the file notes what was asked and when, until Taken next opens, and Taken offers it again if that is within 10 minutes. If you start the 7-day trial on this phone, Taken also notes the day it ends, for its reminder. When you buy, Taken tells Google Play only the plan you picked.

The Google Play Billing Library, which Taken includes to do this, is Google's code. It talks to the Google Play Store app on your phone and to Google, under Google's privacy policy. Google's release notes say the library also reports to Google how its own calls went, such as successes, failures and connection problems, to improve the library and support errors. Taken gives it nothing from your log, and Taken's own code makes no network requests.

Like every publisher on Google Play, we receive Google's sales reports. For each order they list the order number, the date, the plan, the price and tax, the phone model and the buyer's country, and in some countries the buyer's region, city and postal code. The reports hold no name or email address and nothing from your log. We use them only for our accounts, tax and refunds, and never link them to anything else. We keep them for 6 years after the end of the financial year they belong to, as UK company law requires. If you write to us about a charge, Play Console lets us look up that order by its order number or by the email address of the Google account that paid.

Android backup. Taken uses Android's Auto Backup. If backup is on for your phone, Android copies Taken's files to a private folder in your own Google account's Drive, about once a day while the phone is idle and on Wi-Fi. Those files are your stack and log, to-dos, weigh-ins, settings, profile name and photo, and the Pro status file. Taken allows this only when the backup is end-to-end encrypted with your phone's PIN, pattern or password. Android does this on Android 9 and later once a screen lock is set. Without a screen lock, Taken's files are not backed up to the cloud. Android keeps only the latest backup, and restores it when you install Taken again or set up a new phone with the same Google account. Copying your old phone to a new one during setup moves the same files directly between the two phones. We have no access to any of it. To turn backup off for your phone, open Settings, then Google, then All services, tap Backup under Backup and restore, and turn off Backup by Google One. Names differ a little between phones; Google's guide has the details.

Notifications. The nudge, the trial reminder and to-do times are local notifications, scheduled on your phone with Android's alarm service and posted by Android. No push service and no server is involved. Taken uses inexact alarms, so a notification can arrive up to about 10 minutes after its time, or later while the phone is idle and saving battery. On Android 13 and later, Taken asks for notification permission only when you turn the nudge on, in your profile or from the one-time offer on Today, or once after a trial starts. You can always choose Not now. Each kind of notification has its own channel in Android's settings, so you can turn any of them off there. The nudge names what is still pending by its short code, such as VD3, and its Take all button works from the lock screen; your phone's lock screen settings decide whether notification text shows while it is locked.

Voice to-dos. Taken uses only Android's on-device speech recognizer (Android 12 and later), never a recognizer that may send audio to a server. The audio is never stored or sent, and the words are kept only as your to-dos, on your phone. On Android 13 and later, Taken gives the recognizer the same spelling hints as on iPhone. If your phone does not yet have the speech model for your language, Android may download it for next time; that download carries nothing you said. No language model sorts your notes on Android: each note is saved as you said it, one line per sentence. Taken asks for microphone permission the first time you tap the mic. You can turn it off at any time in Android's settings, and type your to-dos instead.

Widgets, the tile and shortcuts. The home-screen widgets, the Take all tile in Quick Settings and Taken's two app shortcuts read and update the same log on your phone. The tile shows only how many are left, never names, because Quick Settings can open over the lock screen, and it can log your stack without unlocking. Taken for Android has no Siri or Shortcuts actions and does not connect to any assistant.

Photos and files. A profile photo comes from Android's photo picker, which hands Taken only the photo you pick; Taken asks for no access to your photos or storage. Export CSV and Export weight CSV use Android's share sheet, and Import weight CSV reads only the file you pick in Android's file picker.

What we do not do. Section 05 holds for Taken's own code on Android. The difference is Google's code. Taken for Android is built with Google's Android libraries and includes the Google Play Billing Library, which is why it has the internet permission. That library reports its own calls to Google, as described above. Beyond that library, Taken includes no analytics, crash reporting, advertising or tracking code, and it does not use the Advertising ID.

Deleting your data. Erase all data works as in section 11, and also cancels Taken's reminders and removes its notifications. Uninstalling Taken deletes everything it stored on the phone. A backup in your Google account is kept and deleted by Google under your backup settings; after Erase all data, the next backup replaces it, since Android keeps only the latest. A Taken Pro purchase belongs to your Google account, and Taken finds it again by itself each time it opens.

13Children

Taken is rated 13+ on the App Store and is meant for people 13 and over on Google Play. It collects no data from anyone, including children under 13 or the minimum age that applies in your country.

14Your rights

Privacy laws such as the UK GDPR, the EU GDPR and the California Consumer Privacy Act give you rights over personal data a company holds about you. Because the app sends us no personal data about you, there is nothing for us to access, correct, export or delete on our side. Your log is on your device, under your control. For a Google Play order, you can ask us about Google's sales record of it (section 12) at the email below.

15This website

This site is hosted on GitHub Pages. It sets no cookies and uses no analytics, trackers or third-party scripts, and its fonts are served from the site itself. GitHub, as the host, may process technical data such as IP addresses to deliver and secure the site, under the GitHub General Privacy Statement.

16Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, we will update this page and the effective date above. If a change is significant, we will also say so in the app's release notes.
